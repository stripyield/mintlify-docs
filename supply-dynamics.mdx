## Farming emissions

The farming schedule starts at **900,000 STRIP per day** and declines exponentially at **0.001 per day**, within a **900 million STRIP farming budget**. The rate halves approximately every 693 days.

The emission clock runs through accumulation and trading. Closing claims changes when rewards can be withdrawn, not the underlying emission schedule.

A pool's allocation is not the same as the tokens a user earns: farming eligibility determines the earned portion.

## Two types of burns

**Buyback burns:** protocol funds purchase STRIP from the market and destroy it.

**Reward-allocation burns:** when gross farming allocations settle, the protocol mints the allocation and immediately burns the portion excluded by historical eligibility. The earned remainder stays in custody for user claims.

For a 1,000 STRIP allocation at 40% eligibility, 600 is burned and 400 becomes the user's eligible reward. The 75% retention rule applies to that 400, not to the burned amount.

Both burns reduce maximum attainable outstanding supply under the lifetime issuance ceiling. Only buyback burns represent market purchases.

## Reading the numbers

Total economic STRIP burned combines both categories, with the breakdown shown separately. Outstanding supply includes minted rewards still held for users; it is not the same as freely circulating supply.

The lifetime ceiling remains 1 billion. A burn reduces the amount that can ever remain outstanding without reopening minting capacity.

Burns do not promise net deflation in any particular period. Compare actual issuance and burns rather than assuming one outweighs the other.
