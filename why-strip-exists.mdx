---
title: "Why Strip Exists"
description: "Yield should coordinate demand, not just pay it out."
---

### Yield-bearing collateral changed the shape of DeFi.

Capital no longer needs to sit idle. Staked assets, liquid restaking tokens, yield-bearing stablecoins, and productive LP positions all generate output while they remain in use. Productive collateral is now the default state of serious onchain capital.

### But almost every system treats that output the same way.

Yield is paid out. Yield is sold. Yield becomes passive income. The value leaves the asset that produced it, leaves the protocol that housed it, and rarely returns as persistent demand. Billions of dollars of recurring output evaporate into individual payouts with no structural destination.

### Strip exists because productive collateral should do more than generate income.

It should coordinate demand.

When supported collateral enters Strip, the depositor keeps a liquid claim on principal through Principal Tokens. The yield is separated from the principal and routed by a fixed rule: half buys and burns STRIP, and half compounds back into the collateral base. The rest of the protocol (emissions, liquidity, boost) is built around directing incentives toward the users who strengthen that loop.

### This changes the role of yield.

It is no longer only a payout to the depositor. It becomes a recurring input into three things at once:

- **Token demand**: routed yield is a persistent, protocol-level bid for STRIP.
- **Collateral growth**: the compounded half enlarges the base that produces the next cycle's yield.
- **Liquidity formation**: incentives pull participants into the markets that connect principal to STRIP.

Strip gives assets that produce value a destination.
