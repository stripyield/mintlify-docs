---
title: "Farming rewards"
---

When you stake PT or an LP receipt, your deposit earns a share of its pool's STRIP allocation. Your reward multiplier grows with the age of that deposit.

## Multiplier

| Time staked since entry or reset | Reward multiplier |
| --- | --- |
| Entry | 1x |
| 30 days | 10.5x |
| 60 days and beyond | 20x |

Your multiplier grows steadily between those points. At 20x, the same deposit earns twenty times its 1x reward rate under otherwise identical pool conditions. Actual rewards also depend on the pool's allocation and your share of its stake.

Adding capital starts a new ramp for that addition. Earlier deposits keep their age. Capital you withdraw loses its age, so staking it again starts fresh.

Your earning rate applies as rewards accrue. Waiting until day 60 to claim does not upgrade rewards from day one. The portion excluded by eligibility is burned when the rewards are settled.

## Retention

Your account has one retention rule across all its PT and LP farming pools: **leave 75% of the eligible rewards earned in your current earning cycle unclaimed**.

Once claims open, the other 25% is available without resetting.

For example, after earning 1,000 STRIP, you can claim 250 and leave 750 unclaimed. Earn another 400 and you can claim another 100 without resetting. The retained balance grows alongside your earnings.

STRIP you have already claimed no longer counts toward that balance, even if you hold it, stake it or use it in an LP position.

## Reset

You can claim beyond your allowance by choosing the explicit claim-and-reset option.

That restarts every remaining PT and LP farming deposit in your account at 1x. A fresh earning cycle begins with a fresh retention requirement. Any rewards left from your old cycle remain separately withdrawable and do not count toward the new one.

Already-earned rewards remain yours. Your principal stays staked unless you separately withdraw it.

## STRIP staking

The ramp and retention rule apply to farming. [Staking STRIP for trading fees](/st-strip) has neither requirement.
