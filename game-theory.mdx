---
title: "Game Theory"
---

Strip's incentives shift as the system matures. What's optimal for a participant in week one is not what's optimal in year five, and the protocol is designed to reward that adaptation. The four phases below describe how participation, emissions, and buybacks rebalance over time.

**Coordination**

PT staking is the sole emission path, lasting the first 30 days after launch. Early participants earn the largest allocations and have the longest window to build boost. This phase establishes initial TVL and sets the alignment baseline for everything that follows.

**Competition**

Emissions move to PT/STRIP liquidity providers, who stake sWLP to earn. Liquidity deepens while emissions are still high. Boost becomes the deciding factor between participants with similar capital, since a max-boosted LP earns 20x what a baseline LP earns from the same position.

**Crossover**

Buybacks begin to exceed new emissions. The system turns net deflationary while still distributing rewards. This is the phase where the model proves itself: STRIP supply contracts even as participation continues to be paid for.

**Scarcity**

Emissions approach zero. Buybacks dominate. STRIP becomes a scarce claim on a perpetually compounding yield base, valued not by what it pays out today but by what the underlying base produces forever.

The arc is intentional. Coordination builds the foundation, competition deepens the market, crossover proves the math, and scarcity is the steady state. Each phase produces conditions that make the next one work.
